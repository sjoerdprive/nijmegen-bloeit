Dirt, outdated paintwork and later additions such as advertising and cladding have a big
influence on how a street looks. A facelift can make a lot of difference, especially when
several buildings are tackled at the same time. Nijmegen Bloeit wants to bring together owners
who are interested and take on the process together with them.

<div class="let-op">

**Good to know**

- This is an **ambition**. Nothing has been arranged or agreed yet.
- **Owners pay part of the costs themselves.** The municipal grant covers 40 to 50 percent, with a maximum per building.
- **A grant is not guaranteed.** The municipality assesses every application and the rules can change.
- Whether a project goes ahead depends on the interest and choices of the owners themselves.

</div>

### The façade grant of the Municipality of Nijmegen

The Municipality of Nijmegen has a grant scheme for refurbishing façades in the post-war
reconstruction area of the city centre. According to the municipality, it applies to buildings
constructed after the Second World War, and the grant is intended for work on the street-facing
façade, such as cleaning, maintenance, painting and removing elements that do not suit the
building.

Applying together pays off. According to the municipality, the contribution is:

| Number of buildings | Grant | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 building | 40% of the costs | €4,000 |
| 2 buildings | 45% per building | €4,500 per building |
| 3 or more buildings | 50% per building | €5,000 per building |

If you work together with other owners, each owner applies separately, with a declaration and
one joint quote for all buildings. Work may only start after the municipality's decision.
